# Persona: P1_adults_65plus

**Who (outer layer only):** age_band = 65+ · **n = 277** (194 train / 83 held out) · source: Twin-2K-500 waves 1–3 · evidence: `P1_adults_65plus_evidence.md`

**Bottom line.** This group is **calmer** (lower neuroticism) and **describes itself more favourably** (higher social desirability) than the panel. Both differences replicate on held-out people and survive all 5 one-criterion redefinitions. On values, money style, reflection, generosity and the hypothetical green choice, treat it as the population average. The earlier 'less reflective' (CRT) result was a scoring artefact and is withdrawn.

**Why it fits.** *Observed:* both traits move steadily across age bands (neuroticism 3.14 → 2.28 from 18–29 to 65+; corrected social desirability 5.75 → 7.61). *Interpretation:* this matches published age differences in emotional stability and socially desirable responding. *Speculation:* ageing, cohort and self-presentation cannot be separated in cross-sectional data. Adjusting for social desirability shrinks the neuroticism gap from −0.50 to −0.31 SD, so part of the calm may be self-description; one self-report scale cannot tell response style from real adjustment.

**Say vs do.** In the hypothetical $39 renewable offer, 43% chose renewable when it required switching (n = 49; population 36%) and 55% when it was the default (n = 40; 58%). Neither differs from the population, and neither predicts better than the population rate on held-out members.

**Where it breaks.** This describes the group, not its members: a random 65+ member is more neurotic than a random 18–29-year-old 25% of the time. These are online-panel 65+ adults, and they are over-represented in the experiments (21% vs 10%).
