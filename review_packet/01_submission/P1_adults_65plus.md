# Persona: P1_adults_65plus

**Who (outer layer only):** age_band = 65+ · **n = 277** (194 train / 83 held out) · source: Twin-2K-500 waves 1–3 · evidence: `P1_adults_65plus_evidence.md`

**Bottom line.** This group is **calmer** (lower neuroticism) and **describes itself more favourably** (higher social desirability) than the panel. Both differences replicate on held-out people and survive all 5 one-criterion redefinitions. On values, money style, reflection, generosity and the hypothetical green choice, treat it as the population average. The earlier 'less reflective' (CRT) result was a scoring artefact and is withdrawn.

**Why this persona.** It asks what age alone reveals. P3 sits at the other end of the same gradient.

**Extended profile (exploratory).** Training members only (n = 194), no held-out check; six further scales re-derived from items. Higher self-concept clarity (d +0.43), agreeableness (+0.34), conscientiousness (+0.30) and extraversion (+0.23), all CIs excluding 0; openness (+0.07) and need for cognition (+0.10) unchanged. CIs: `research_frontier/persona_extension/`.

**Why it fits.** *Observed:* both traits move steadily across age bands (neuroticism 3.14 → 2.28 from 18–29 to 65+; corrected social desirability 5.75 → 7.61). *Interpretation:* longitudinal studies find emotional stability, conscientiousness and agreeableness rising across adulthood (Roberts et al. 2006); self-concept clarity is higher in mid-life than in youth (Lodi-Smith & Roberts 2010). *Speculation:* ageing, cohort and self-presentation cannot be separated in cross-sectional data. Adjusting for social desirability shrinks the neuroticism gap from −0.50 to −0.31 SD, so part of the calm may be self-description.

**Say vs do.** In the hypothetical $39 renewable offer, 43% chose renewable when it required switching (n = 49; population 36%) and 55% when it was the default (n = 40; 58%). These small samples did not establish a difference from the population, on training or held-out members (held out: 25 and 18); that is not evidence of equivalence.

**Where it breaks.** This describes the group, not its members: a random 65+ member is more neurotic than a random 18–29-year-old 25% of the time. **Inside it:** the 40 members with income under $30,000 are no calmer than the population (neuroticism d −0.09, CI −0.40 to 0.22; other 65+ −0.52). Found by inspection.
